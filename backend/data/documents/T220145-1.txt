# STUDY_ID: T220145-1
# COMPOUND: BAY-45-A
# SPECIES: DOG
# ROUTE: Oral capsule
# TITLE: 13-Week Oral Toxicity Study of BAY-45-A in Beagle Dogs

@@PAGE 1
@@SECTION 1 Summary
This GLP study assessed the toxicity of BAY-45-A administered orally by capsule to Beagle
dogs for 13 weeks. Groups of 4 males and 4 females received 0, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg/day.
The NOAEL was 10 mg/kg/day, based on gastrointestinal signs and cardiovascular effects at
30 mg/kg/day.

@@PAGE 40
@@SECTION 3.2 Clinical Signs
At 30 mg/kg/day, emesis and salivation were observed in the majority of animals within
30 minutes to 2 hours after dosing. Emesis occurred in 3 of 4 males and 4 of 4 females.
Salivation was transient and considered secondary to the emetic response. No clinical signs
were observed at 3 or 10 mg/kg/day.

@@PAGE 62
@@SECTION 3.5 Cardiovascular Safety (ECG)
Electrocardiographic evaluation revealed a treatment-related prolongation of the QT interval
at 30 mg/kg/day, with a mean increase of approximately 8% relative to baseline at the peak
plasma concentration timepoint. The heart-rate-corrected QT (QTcV) was similarly increased.
No QT effects were observed at 3 or 10 mg/kg/day. There were no changes in PR or QRS intervals.

@@PAGE 88
@@SECTION 3.8 Histopathology
No test-item-related microscopic findings were observed in the heart or any other organ at
any dose level. The QT prolongation at 30 mg/kg/day was therefore considered a functional
electrophysiological effect without structural correlate.

@@PAGE 95
@@SECTION 4 Conclusion
The NOAEL for BAY-45-A in the Beagle dog under these conditions was 10 mg/kg/day, based on
emesis, salivation, and QT interval prolongation observed at 30 mg/kg/day.
