@@SECTION 1 Summary
This Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) study evaluated the systemic toxicity of BAY-45-A
administered daily by oral gavage to Wistar Han rats for 13 consecutive weeks, followed
by a 4-week treatment-free recovery period. Four groups of 10 males and 10 females
received 0 (vehicle control), 25, 75, or 150 mg/kg/day. The no-observed-adverse-effect
level (NOAEL) was established at 25 mg/kg/day based on clinical signs, hepatocellular
hypertrophy, and reversible elevations in serum transaminases at higher dose levels.

@@PAGE 12
@@SECTION 2.1 Materials and Methods - Test System
Healthy young adult Wistar Han rats were obtained from a licensed supplier and acclimatized
for 14 days prior to dosing. Animals were housed under controlled conditions (22 +/- 3 C,
12-hour light/dark cycle) with free access to standard rodent diet and water. The dose
formulations were prepared in 0.5% aqueous methylcellulose vehicle and administered at a
constant volume of 10 mL/kg once daily.

@@PAGE 44
@@SECTION 3.2 Clinical Signs
Detailed clinical observations were recorded daily throughout the treatment and recovery
periods. At 150 mg/kg/day, treatment-related clinical signs included piloerection, ataxia,
and eyes partially closed, observed predominantly within 1 to 3 hours after dosing from
study week 4 onward. Loose faeces was noted intermittently in high-dose females during
weeks 6 to 13. These signs were transient, resolving before the next daily dose, and were
not observed at 25 or 75 mg/kg/day. No clinical signs of toxicological significance were
observed in the control, 25, or 75 mg/kg/day groups. During the 4-week recovery period,
all clinical signs resolved completely, indicating reversibility.

@@PAGE 45
@@SECTION 3.2 Clinical Signs
A summary of the incidence of clinical signs at 150 mg/kg/day is provided below.
Piloerection was observed in 8 of 10 males and 9 of 10 females. Ataxia was observed in
6 of 10 males and 7 of 10 females. Eyes partially closed was observed in 4 of 10 males
and 5 of 10 females. Loose faeces was observed in 3 of 10 males and 6 of 10 females. No
salivation, tremors, or convulsions were observed at any dose level.

@@PAGE 58
@@SECTION 3.4 Body Weight and Food Consumption
Mean body weight gain was reduced by approximately 12% in high-dose (150 mg/kg/day) males
relative to controls over the treatment period. Food consumption was correspondingly lower
in the high-dose group during weeks 1 to 6, after which it recovered. Body weight and food
consumption were unaffected at 25 and 75 mg/kg/day.

@@PAGE 71
@@SECTION 3.6 Clinical Pathology
Serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) were elevated in
a dose-dependent manner at 75 and 150 mg/kg/day, reaching approximately 2.1-fold and
2.8-fold the control mean, respectively, at the high dose at the end of treatment. These
changes correlated with hepatocellular hypertrophy on histopathology. By the end of the
4-week recovery period, ALT and AST had returned to values comparable to controls,
demonstrating reversibility. Haematology parameters were unremarkable at all dose levels.

@@PAGE 96
@@SECTION 3.8 Histopathology
Microscopic examination revealed centrilobular hepatocellular hypertrophy in the liver of
males and females at 75 and 150 mg/kg/day. The change was graded minimal to moderate and
was considered an adaptive response consistent with hepatic enzyme induction. No hepatocellular
necrosis, inflammation, or fibrosis was observed. Thyroid, kidney, and other organs examined
were within normal limits. At the end of the recovery period, hepatocellular hypertrophy had
substantially resolved.

@@PAGE 110
@@SECTION 4 Conclusion
Under the conditions of this study, daily oral administration of BAY-45-A to Wistar rats for
13 weeks was associated with adaptive hepatocellular hypertrophy and reversible transaminase
elevation at 75 and 150 mg/kg/day, and transient clinical signs (piloerection, ataxia, eyes
partially closed, loose faeces) at 150 mg/kg/day. All findings were reversible following a
4-week recovery period. The NOAEL was determined to be 25 mg/kg/day.
